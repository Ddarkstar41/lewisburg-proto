## What's proposed

A group of developers proposes to build a number of upscale homes on the Lewisburg waterfront by
**bulkheading and filling** areas that are currently open water. The work would convert natural
shoreline, marsh, and water bottoms into private residential lots.

## Why the association opposes it

**Habitat.** The water bottoms and marsh slated for filling are breeding and feeding grounds for
birds, fish, and other wildlife. Once bulkheaded and filled, they are gone — this is not a change
that can be reversed later if the outcome proves worse than predicted.

**Truck traffic.** Filling open water takes an enormous volume of material. That means thousands of
heavy truck deliveries along Northlake Drive, Holly Street, and Copal Street — narrow parish roads
with no sidewalks, where people walk, cycle, and push strollers in the road because there is
nowhere else to go.

**Flooding and erosion.** Bulkheads and fill change how water moves. The concern is that during
high-water events the Bayou Chinchuba watershed will hold and shed water differently, worsening
flooding and erosion for properties that have nothing to do with the development.

## Where it stands

The developers withdrew an initial application and resubmitted it as permit **P20230099**. The most
recent public comment period has closed. The association is monitoring for a decision.
